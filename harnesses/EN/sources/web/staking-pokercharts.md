# Staking, makeup and selling action (PokerCharts)

Source: PokerCharts blog, Staking, Makeup, and Selling Action: A Plain-English Guide
URL: https://pokercharts.com/en/blog/poker-staking-makeup-explained
Checked: 2026-09-23
Evidence: Worked makeup example, the difference between staking and selling action, a rule of thumb for fair markup, and the warning that staking disputes are usually record-keeping disputes.

- Makeup example: two sessions lose $500 each, so makeup is $1,000. A $1,300 win first clears the $1,000, then the remaining $300 is split 50/50, so the player gets $150 and the backer gets $1,000 back plus $150.
- Selling action is pre-selling pieces of one tournament to several buyers, unlike a backer funding everything over time.
- Rule of thumb: fair markup is about 1 plus half the player's ROI. A 20% ROI player might justify 1.10; 1.30 or more needs a strong, proven record.
- Most staking disputes come from poor records, not dishonesty, so keep shared written records of buy-ins, results and makeup.
