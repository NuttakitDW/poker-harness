# Poker staking essentials (GTO Wizard)

Source: GTO Wizard blog, Poker Staking Essentials
URL: https://blog.gtowizard.com/poker-staking-essentials/
Checked: 2026-09-23
Evidence: Explains selling action with markup, makeup backing deals, swaps, why players sell action to cut variance, and the trust, transparency and tax risks of staking.

- Selling action: a player sells percentages of a tournament buy-in. At 1.2 markup, 10% of a $1,000 entry costs the buyer $120 instead of $100. Buyers get their percentage of any prize; the player keeps the markup premium.
- Players tend to overestimate their ROI when setting markup. One view is to split the edge: a 40% ROI player charging 1.2 leaves both sides about 20% of the expected value.
- Reasons to sell action: moving up in stakes, taking shots at bigger or softer games, learning a new stake, or selling equity at a final table.
- Makeup deals: the backer funds 100% of buy-ins and profit is usually split 50/50. Losses build up as makeup that must be won back before any split. The backer is repaid first and only profit above makeup is split. Makeup clears only when the player is back in profit or the deal ends. Some deals pay a small wage from big scores while makeup stays.
- Markup deals are one-off: no future obligation if the player loses.
- Swaps: friends trade small pieces (5-10%) of each other's results.
- Risks: non-payment by either side, overselling (more than 100% sold), undisclosed tax, travel costs or perks, misrepresenting ability, and stables sharing data or colluding. Tax on winnings depends on country; agree whether backers are paid before or after tax. Gambling staking contracts are usually hard to enforce in court.
