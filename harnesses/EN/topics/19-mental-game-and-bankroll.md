# Mental game, performance and bankroll

- Stable ID: `19-mental-game-and-bankroll`
- Scope: Poker bankroll and personal finances should be kept distinct; no universal bankroll number is implied.
- Thai counterpart: [จิตใจ สมรรถนะ และเงินทุน](../../TH/topics/19-mental-game-and-bankroll.md)

## Core idea

Variance can make good decisions lose in the short run. Record decision quality separately from outcomes. Before a session, set a time and loss limit, check sleep and attention, and identify emotional triggers. During play, pause when anger, fear or urgency narrows reasoning; afterward review specific hands and the trigger behind repeated mistakes. Choose stakes so ordinary downswings do not threaten essential expenses, and move down when funds or performance no longer support the current game. Bankroll policy depends on format, edge, variance and personal risk tolerance.

## Routine and risk controls

**Before:** define the game and stakes, a planned session length, a break schedule, and the emotional signs that will trigger a pause. Check sleep and attention; tired play can make even known strategy inaccessible. **During:** note the trigger (“lost a large pot after a bad beat”), label the emotion, take a short break and return to the decision process only when you can state ranges and prices again. **After:** review key hands away from the table, distinguish a correct high-variance call from a mistaken call, and practice the leak that recurs.

A loss limit can protect attention and essential funds but is not a proof that every additional hand is negative EV. Likewise, a win target does not alter the EV of the next fair bet. Choose a bankroll buffer for the specific variance and personal obligations; never treat “double up then leave” as a mathematical guarantee or as changing a fair coin flip’s expectation.

## Worked example (authored; not quoted from source)

Authored example: Losing three all-ins as a favorite is not itself evidence of poor play; check ranges and equity assumptions before changing strategy.

## Practice and boundary checks

Bankroll decisions and session decisions have different time scales. A bankroll rule chooses a sustainable game before play; a session stop rule protects attention or a personal limit during play. Neither turns an unfavorable wager favorable. Review tilt by its specific pattern: anger after a bad beat, fear of losing profit, overconfidence after a win, or urgency to recover losses call for different corrective routines. Monitor signs such as speeding up decisions or abandoning range estimates. The goal is not to suppress emotion perfectly; it is to keep emotion from silently changing betting decisions.

## Common errors

Chasing losses at higher stakes; using session profit as the sole measure of decision quality.
