# Cash games, microstakes and six-max

- Stable ID: `12-cash-microstakes-sixmax`
- Scope: No-limit hold’em; source-era player pools and rake assumptions may be outdated.
- Thai counterpart: [เกมเงินสด ไมโครสเตก และโต๊ะหกคน](../../TH/topics/12-cash-microstakes-sixmax.md)

## Core idea

Cash-game chips usually map directly to money, and players can often rebuy; choose decisions by chip EV after rake and any table-specific rules. Six-max has fewer seats before the blinds return, so position and blind defense frequencies differ from full-ring. At small stakes, classify tendencies from actual samples: calling too much, folding too much, or betting too little are distinct leaks. Against habitual callers, prioritize clear value and avoid automatic low-equity bluffs. Track rake, effective stack and sample size before importing population advice from older books.

## Microstakes review loop

Record a concrete tendency before exploiting: for example, “called 8 of 10 river bets after reaching showdown” is more useful than “loose.” Separate passive callers from aggressive bluffers and tight folders. Against passive callers, value bet hands ahead of their calling range and avoid bluffing hands that lack fold equity. Against frequent folders, choose bluffs that retain reasonable blockers or equity. Six-max increases blind frequency and late-position opportunities, but rake can make marginal calls unprofitable.

Authored comparison: A button steal may look profitable against blinds that each fold 70%, but if one blind begins three-betting often, the opening EV and continuation plan must be recalculated.

## Worked example (authored; not quoted from source)

Authored example: If an opponent calls every river bet with any pair, thin value bets improve while a missed-draw pure bluff loses its usual fold equity.

## Practice and boundary checks

The microstakes label describes a buy-in level, not an opponent type. Some players are recreational callers, some are disciplined regulars, and some have limited evidence. Use observations to distinguish them. In six-max, fewer seats mean you face blind obligations more often, but do not infer that every late-position hand should be opened. Check the players in blinds, rake and available stacks. A cash-game win rate is noisy over small samples; use hand reviews and objective statistics before concluding that a single strategy change helped. Historic source recommendations should be tested against the current table rather than assumed to transfer.

## Common errors

Assuming all microstakes opponents play alike; ignoring rake in small pots.
