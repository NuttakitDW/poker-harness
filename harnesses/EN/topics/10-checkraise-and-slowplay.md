# Check-raise, protection and slowplay

- Stable ID: `10-checkraise-and-slowplay`
- Scope: No-limit postflop; opponent betting frequency and board texture matter.
- Thai counterpart: [เช็กเรส การป้องกัน และการซ่อนมือ](../../TH/topics/10-checkraise-and-slowplay.md)

## Core idea

A check-raise lets an opponent bet first, then increases the price of continuing. It can build value with strong hands or create fold equity with selected draws. Before checking, ask whether the opponent is likely to bet; otherwise a value opportunity may disappear. Slowplaying keeps weak hands in the pot but gives free cards. It is more defensible when your hand is hard to outdraw, the board is static and an opponent may bluff later. On dynamic boards, immediate betting or raising often protects equity and grows the pot.

## Choose the reason before choosing the trap

A value check-raise needs both a likely opposing bet and worse hands willing to continue against the raise. A bluff check-raise needs folds or enough equity when called. A protection raise has value when many future cards can reverse the hand, but raising can also isolate you against stronger hands.

Authored contrast: Top set on A♦7♣2♥ is hard to outdraw and can sometimes check if an aggressive opponent often stabs. Top set on J♠T♠9♦ leaves many straight and flush runouts; betting immediately often captures calls from draws. Neither line is automatic: effective stacks and opponent tendencies decide.

Authored example: A set on J♠T♠9♦ faces many straight and flush runouts; checking solely to hide strength can be costly.

## Practice and boundary checks

A slowplay can gain extra bluffs later, but its EV also depends on how many bad turn cards arrive and whether the opponent would have called a bet now. It is particularly risky when several opponents can see a free card. A check-raise changes pot geometry sharply: after raising, plan whether a turn shove is possible and which hands can continue. Check-raising only monsters makes the line transparent; including selected draws can give it coverage, but adding every draw can overbluff. The frequency should follow the board and the opponent’s continuation range.

## Common errors

Assuming the opponent always bets after a check; raising every strong hand on every board.
