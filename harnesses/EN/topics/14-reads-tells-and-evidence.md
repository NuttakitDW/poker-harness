# Opponent reads and tells

- Stable ID: `14-reads-tells-and-evidence`
- Scope: Live and online observations; inference should be tied to actions and sample size.
- Thai counterpart: [การอ่านคู่ต่อสู้และหลักฐาน](../../TH/topics/14-reads-tells-and-evidence.md)

## Core idea

Start with observable facts: positions, sizes, timing, shown hands and repeated lines. Infer a range, then update when new actions occur. A live physical tell can be noisy or deliberately reversed; an online timing pattern may reflect multitabling or connection delay. Give more weight to repeated betting behavior and showdowns than to one gesture or one hand. Mark uncertain reads as hypotheses and choose an exploit only when the expected gain outweighs the risk of a mistaken classification.

## Observation ledger

Write observations in a reproducible form: “button opened 7 of 9 opportunities” is evidence; “button is wild” is interpretation. Note the denominator and whether the opportunities were comparable. Prioritize shown hands because they reveal what a player actually brought through a betting line, but remember one shown hand is only one sample.

For live play, separate involuntary-looking tells from betting-line information and never assume a tell is stable without repetition. For online play, timing and bet presets can be affected by multitabling, automation and connection lag. A useful exploit names the action it changes: “value bet thinner because this player called three large river bets with second pair,” while retaining uncertainty until more hands accumulate.

## Worked example (authored; not quoted from source)

Authored example: Three river overbets shown as nuts suggest strength but are a tiny sample; preserve bluff candidates in the range until more evidence appears.

## Practice and boundary checks

Do not collapse a read into a single probability without a sample model. Seeing two bluffs in two shown river hands does not prove a 100% bluff rate because many unshown hands were folded or never reached river. Showdown selection is biased toward hands that were called. When a tell conflicts with the betting line, give priority to the full line until the tell has repeated predictive value. A concise note should preserve time and context: “river overbet after missed flush, showed value once,” rather than “always strong.”

## Common errors

Treating one physical tell as proof; translating a timing pattern directly into an exact hand.
