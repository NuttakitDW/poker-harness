# Draw, lowball and triple draw

- Stable ID: `18-draw-lowball-triple-draw`
- Scope: Variant rules differ: ace-to-five and deuce-to-seven assign different value to aces, straights and flushes.
- Thai counterpart: [เกมจั่ว Lowball และ Triple Draw](../../TH/topics/18-draw-lowball-triple-draw.md)

## Core idea

In draw games, players exchange cards, so discard count and betting sequence carry information. Five-card draw high seeks ordinary high hands. In ace-to-five lowball, A-2-3-4-5 is the best low and straights/flushes do not count against low. In deuce-to-seven lowball, aces are high and straights/flushes are bad; 7-5-4-3-2 with mixed suits is the best low. Triple draw allows three draw rounds. Position and whether a player stands pat or draws one or more cards change likely ranges. The supplied Super/System 2 has a dedicated deuce-to-seven triple-draw chapter; rules for other draw forms appear in broader material.

## Lowball ranking checklist

- Confirm whether the game is high draw, ace-to-five low, deuce-to-seven low, single draw or triple draw. The name changes hand values and number of betting rounds.
- In ace-to-five, aces are low and straights/flushes are ignored for the low. In deuce-to-seven, aces are high and straights/flushes count against the low; a seven-low is the best possible.
- Count how many cards each player discards at each draw. Standing pat can represent a made hand or, in some lines, a bluff. Later draws and bets refine this read.
- Compare the cost of another draw with the likelihood of making a winning low against the opponent’s likely pat or one-card range.

Authored comparison: A-2-3-4-5 is the nut ace-to-five low, but in deuce-to-seven it includes an ace and a straight, so it is a poor low.

## Worked example (authored; not quoted from source)

Authored example: 7-5-4-3-2 suited as a flush is not the best deuce-to-seven low; mixed suits avoid the flush.

## Practice and boundary checks

In draw poker, the number of exchanged cards acts like a public signal but is not proof of a particular hand. A one-card draw can represent several different starting shapes; a pat hand can be strong or a bluff. Compare opponent betting before and after each draw with the cards they took. For deuce-to-seven, do not call a low “smooth” solely because its top card is seven: the next highest ranks decide ties. A 7-5 low is stronger than a 7-6 low when other ranks permit, and breaking a weak pat hand to draw can be correct depending on price and opponent range.

## Common errors

Using ace-to-five rankings in deuce-to-seven; ignoring exposed draw counts.
