# Staking, backing and selling action

- Scope: Selling action with markup, makeup backing deals and swaps as ways to share variance, with the maths and the risks.
- Thai counterpart: [Staking การขายหุ้นและการหาคนแบ็ก](../../TH/topics/28-staking-and-selling-action.md)

## Core idea

Staking means sharing a player's results with investors so no one carries all the variance. In selling action the player sells percentages of a tournament buy-in, often at a markup: at 1.2, 10% of a $1,000 event costs $120 and returns 10% of any prize. Markup should reflect a proven edge; a common rule is 1 plus half the ROI, so a 20% ROI player charges about 1.10. Buyers only profit if the player's real ROI beats the markup, and most players overestimate their ROI. In a makeup deal a backer funds all buy-ins and profit is usually split 50/50, but losses become makeup that the player must win back before seeing any share. Swaps trade small pieces (5-10%) between friends. Selling action reduces variance and lets a player take a shot at bigger buy-ins without risking the whole bankroll, at the cost of giving up part of the upside. Risks: unpaid debts, overselling beyond 100%, unclear tax or expenses, and poor records; agree terms in writing and keep shared records. In Thai, "ขายหุ้น" at the poker table usually means selling action, not selling stocks.

## Worked example (authored; not quoted from source)

A player with a $10,000 bankroll wants to play a $2,000 event. Selling 50% at 1.1 markup brings in $1,100, so the player risks only $900 of their own money and still keeps half the prize. If they finish 10th for $12,000, backers get $6,000 and the player keeps $6,000. Under a 50/50 makeup deal instead, a player $3,000 in makeup who wins $5,000 repays the $3,000 first and splits the remaining $2,000, taking home $1,000.
