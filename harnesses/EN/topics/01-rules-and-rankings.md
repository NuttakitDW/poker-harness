# Rules, showdown and hand ranks

- Stable ID: `01-rules-and-rankings`
- Scope: No-limit Texas hold’em; standard high-hand ranking. House rules control blinds, straddles, rake and odd chips.
- Thai counterpart: [กติกา การเปิดไพ่ และลำดับไพ่](../../TH/topics/01-rules-and-rankings.md)

## Core idea

Each player receives two private cards. Five community cards arrive as flop (three), turn and river. Make the best five-card hand from any zero, one or two hole cards. Action normally runs preflop, flop, turn and river, with a betting round at each stage. A player can win when everyone folds or by showing the best hand among remaining players. In a standard high game, ranks descend: straight flush, four of a kind, full house, flush, straight, three of a kind, two pair, one pair, high card. An ace may be low in A-2-3-4-5, but cannot wrap around in Q-K-A-2-3. Compare the rank-defining cards first, then kickers; suits do not break a tie.

## Distinctions and decision checks

- **Before showdown:** fold ends eligibility for the pot, while a check is available only when no unmatched wager remains. A call matches the price; a raise increases it. In no-limit play a player may commit all remaining chips, but side pots can arise when stacks differ.
- **At showdown:** compare exactly five cards. A flush beats a straight even if the straight has an ace. A full house compares the three-of-a-kind rank first, then its pair. Two players may use the same board five cards; the pot then splits unless one can build a stronger five-card hand with a hole card.
- **Variant boundary:** Omaha requires exactly two private cards, so the Hold’em board-alone example does not transfer. In lowball, even the meaning of a “best” hand changes.

Authored check: Board K♣K♦5♠5♥2♣, Player A holds A♠Q♠ and Player B holds Q♥J♥. Both have two pair, kings and fives, but A’s ace kicker wins.

## Worked example (authored; not quoted from source)

Authored example: Board A♠ K♠ Q♠ J♠ T♠ gives every player the same royal flush if no variant-specific requirement changes hand construction. The pot is split among live hands.

## Practice and boundary checks

A useful hand-reading exercise is to compare the best five cards for two players without naming a hand too early. List the board, then each player’s highest available five-card set; only afterward assign a category and kicker. This prevents the common mistake of using six cards or counting both hole cards when the board already supplies a stronger hand. If a player is all-in before the river, the remaining board cards are still dealt under the table’s procedure; no later decision is available to that player. When ties or side pots arise, separate *eligibility for each pot* from *rank of the hand*.

## Common errors

Forgetting that the board alone can be the best hand; using a suit as a tie-breaker.
