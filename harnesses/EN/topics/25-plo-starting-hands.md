# PLO starting hands and preflop decisions

- Stable ID: `25-plo-starting-hands`
- Scope: Tiering four-card Pot-Limit Omaha starting hands and choosing fold, limp, call or raise preflop with Jeff Hwang's Big Play framework.
- Thai counterpart: [PLO มือเริ่มต้นและการตัดสินใจ preflop](../../TH/topics/25-plo-starting-hands.md)

## Core idea

Every PLO starting hand sorts into Premium, Speculative, Marginal or Trash. The test: imagine the hand's miracle flop, then ask whether that outcome is really the nuts and how often a nearby flop arrives. Because you must use exactly two hole cards, value lives in structure — how all four cards combine into nut straights, nut flush draws, big wraps and top sets. Pretty cards that do not work together are worth little.

Premium: coordinated high cards, double-suited aces, big double pairs such as QQJJ, premium rundowns such as JT98 double-suited; raise, many from any position; AAJT double-suited (the Magnum) is the best. Speculative: big-play potential with a structural flaw (low cards, non-nut draws, gaps); a calling hand, raise the bigger ones late. Marginal: one-way hands (lone pair, three-card hand, top-gap like KJT9); late position for the minimum only. Trash: dominated or second-nut draws, uncoordinated "two hold'em hands" such as QJ76, low pairs that cannot make top set; fold.

Demoting flaws: low straights get out-flopped (9753 is Trash), non-nut flushes, a dangler that turns the hand into a three-card hand, a top gap. Not all aces are equal: double-suited AA with connectors raises anywhere; bare AA82 rainbow is only Speculative. Facing a raise and a reraise, do not cold-call two bets just because you hold aces.

## Worked example (authored; not quoted from source)

KKQJ double-suited is Premium: T98 flops the nut straight with redraws and both big pairs and both suits work together. QJ76 looks playable but is two unconnected hold'em hands, so it is Trash.
