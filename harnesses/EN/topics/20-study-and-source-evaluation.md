## Core idea

Study a hand by writing positions, effective stacks, pot, board, actions and candidate ranges before checking an answer. Calculate combos, pot odds and EV independently; then compare with workbook solutions and explain disagreements. Group errors by concept, revisit them after a delay, and test decisions against fresh hands. The Markdown corpus is page-faithful extraction: text can miss diagrams, tables and equations. Open the cited PDF page when a layout or symbol matters. Treat historical tactical advice as evidence about that source’s game conditions, not a timeless chart.

## A repeatable study drill

Choose one workbook problem and hide the answer. Write known cards, pot before action, amount to call, effective stacks and exact question. Work the calculation by hand; for a combo problem remove visible blockers, and for pot odds distinguish call equity from bluff fold frequency. Record both the numerical answer and the assumptions required for it. Only then compare the key and investigate disagreements. If OCR shows a strange card or missing suit, open the PDF page; extracted characters can be wrong.

After the drill, find a fresh hand that uses the same concept. Explain the action in one sentence to a hypothetical beginner. A remembered answer to one worksheet is less useful than a method that survives changed pot sizes, positions and opponents.

## Worked example (authored; not quoted from source)

Authored example: Compute 12 offsuit and 4 suited AK combos before opening a workbook key; then test how an exposed ace changes both counts.

## Practice and boundary checks

A source citation should be specific enough for another agent to inspect: book title, physical PDF page, and the assumption taken from that page. The page-faithful Markdown chunks preserve ordering but cannot guarantee equations, diagrams or suits survived extraction. For any numerical claim, independently verify arithmetic and inspect the original image when notation is damaged. Separate the source’s worked example from a new example you construct. If two books disagree, compare game type, publication era, rake, stack depth and opponent model before choosing which advice applies.

## Common errors

Reading an answer without reconstructing the spot; trusting OCR for a diagram or mathematical sign.
