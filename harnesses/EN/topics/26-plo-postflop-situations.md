# PLO post-flop situations

- Stable ID: `26-plo-postflop-situations`
- Scope: Four-card PLO post-flop bet, raise, call or fold decisions by hand, board, action and stacks, from Jeff Hwang's Chapter 6 spots.
- Thai counterpart: [PLO สถานการณ์หลัง flop](../../TH/topics/26-plo-postflop-situations.md)

## Core idea

Do not slow-play monsters: the nut straight with redraws, a set with a big draw or a full house should bet, because boards change and draws arrive. Big draws want aggression: 13–17 card wraps and nut flush draws are often favourites and carry fold equity, but only draw to the nut end; sucker wraps and non-nut flush draws are traps. Respect the nuts: second set, top two, bottom set, top-and-bottom and non-nut full houses lose stacks to a bet and a raise on coordinated or paired boards; disciplined folds are most of the edge. Weak action (blinds check, a weak stab, flat calls) invites a raise in position. Every answer depends on the exact action, stacks and opponents described.

## Worked example (authored; not quoted from source)

QJT9 double-suited on 876 two-tone flops the nut straight with redraws: bet the pot. Second set of kings on AK7 facing a big bet deep-stacked is a fold, since the bettor pays off fully only with aces.
