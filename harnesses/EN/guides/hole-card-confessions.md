## Explained study themes

### Information and player types

Owen Gaines treats hands as an information contest: collect actions, infer why someone plays, then classify tendencies without claiming to know exact hole cards.

Evidence: [PDF p. 20](../../../sources/pdf/Hole%20Card%20Confessions.pdf#page=20) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/hole-card-confessions/pages-0017-0024.md#pdf-page-20)

### Range construction and notes

The range chapter builds and reshapes plausible hand sets; Eagle Eyes turns specific observations and notes into later decisions instead of broad labels alone.

Evidence: [PDF p. 58](../../../sources/pdf/Hole%20Card%20Confessions.pdf#page=58) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/hole-card-confessions/pages-0057-0064.md#pdf-page-58)

### Steal equity and range exploitation

Enter Steal Equity discusses ways to win pots without showdown, including position and limps; Exploiting a Range uses accumulated actions to predict how the opponent responds to bets.

Evidence: [PDF p. 72](../../../sources/pdf/Hole%20Card%20Confessions.pdf#page=72) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/hole-card-confessions/pages-0065-0072.md#pdf-page-72)

### Advanced reading and field trip

Later chapters cover thinking levels, implied odds, paying for information, unknowns, and aggression. A Field Trip applies the method to hands; quizzes and answers support self-checking.

Evidence: [PDF p. 116](../../../sources/pdf/Hole%20Card%20Confessions.pdf#page=116) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/hole-card-confessions/pages-0113-0120.md#pdf-page-116)
