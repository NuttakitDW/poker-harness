## Explained study themes

### Expectation and structure

Sklansky starts with EV/hourly rate, antes, odds, and the effect of many opponents. The book spans poker forms; verify each example's game and bet structure before applying it to NLHE.

Evidence: [PDF p. 14](../../../sources/pdf/The%20Theory%20of%20Poker%20%28Seventh%20printing%2C%20Complete%29%20%28David%20Sklansky%29.pdf#page=14) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/the-theory-of-poker/pages-0009-0016.md#pdf-page-14)

### Odds and deception

Effective and implied odds account for future streets; reverse implied losses arise when a draw makes a second-best hand. Deception's value depends on what opponents can notice and adjust to.

Evidence: [PDF p. 39](../../../sources/pdf/The%20Theory%20of%20Poker%20%28Seventh%20printing%2C%20Complete%29%20%28David%20Sklansky%29.pdf#page=39) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/the-theory-of-poker/pages-0033-0040.md#pdf-page-39)

### Fundamental theorem and big pots

The core idea compares an opponent's action with what they would do if they saw your cards; large pots amplify the cost of a wrong fold or call. Multiway pots complicate that simple comparison.

Evidence: [PDF p. 51](../../../sources/pdf/The%20Theory%20of%20Poker%20%28Seventh%20printing%2C%20Complete%29%20%28David%20Sklansky%29.pdf#page=51) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/the-theory-of-poker/pages-0049-0056.md#pdf-page-51) · section start unverified; inspect cited page

### Semi-bluff, raising, check-raising, slowplay

Later chapters distinguish a bluff with drawing equity from pure bluff, list multiple purposes of a raise, specify conditions for check-raising, and explain when slowplay sacrifices less value than it gains.

Evidence: [PDF p. 88](../../../sources/pdf/The%20Theory%20of%20Poker%20%28Seventh%20printing%2C%20Complete%29%20%28David%20Sklansky%29.pdf#page=88) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/the-theory-of-poker/pages-0081-0088.md#pdf-page-88)

### Loose and tight games

Strategy changes with field looseness: the chance of getting called alters semibluff value, while legitimate made hands can gain value in loose games.
