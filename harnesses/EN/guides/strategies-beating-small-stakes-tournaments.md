## Explained study themes

### Opponent-first method

Jonathan Little argues against a fixed chart or one style for every small-stakes tournament. Observe actions even when out of the hand and adjust to the players present.

Evidence: [PDF p. 11](../../../sources/pdf/Strategies%20for%20Beating%20Small%20Stakes%20Poker%20Tournaments..pdf#page=11) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/strategies-beating-small-stakes-tournaments/pages-0009-0016.md#pdf-page-11)

### Loose-passive and loose-aggressive

The book distinguishes opponents who enter too many pots and call too much from those who over-raise. Extract value from excessive calls; avoid confusing wide aggression with strong ranges.

Evidence: [PDF p. 16](../../../sources/pdf/Strategies%20for%20Beating%20Small%20Stakes%20Poker%20Tournaments..pdf#page=16) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/strategies-beating-small-stakes-tournaments/pages-0009-0016.md#pdf-page-16)

### Tight styles and sound opponents

Players who enter too few hands can still differ sharply in passivity or aggression. The author also recognizes opponents playing roughly sound ranges, for whom crude population exploits need stronger evidence.

Evidence: [PDF p. 32](../../../sources/pdf/Strategies%20for%20Beating%20Small%20Stakes%20Poker%20Tournaments..pdf#page=32) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/strategies-beating-small-stakes-tournaments/pages-0025-0032.md#pdf-page-32)

### Stack-sensitive examples

The introduction contrasts deep-stack speculative hands with a 30bb stack, then shows how top pair can be a turn check against a tight range but a value bet against a loose caller. Treat its ROI claims as illustrative, not guarantees.
