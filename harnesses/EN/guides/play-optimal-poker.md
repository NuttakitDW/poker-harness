## Explained study themes

### Equilibrium foundations

Andrew Brokos defines strategies, expected value, Nash equilibrium, indifference, and why genuine randomization is difficult for humans; the toy scenarios train these ideas before poker applications.

Evidence: [PDF p. 18](../../../sources/pdf/Play%20Optimal%20Poker.pdf#page=18) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/play-optimal-poker/pages-0017-0024.md#pdf-page-18)

### Polarized and condensed ranges

The Clairvoyance Game shows how strong hands plus bluffs interact with bluff-catchers and how indifference sets a defending frequency. The frequency belongs to the model's assumptions, not every live spot.

Evidence: [PDF p. 46](../../../sources/pdf/Play%20Optimal%20Poker.pdf#page=46) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/play-optimal-poker/pages-0041-0048.md#pdf-page-46)

### Reciprocal ranges and reality

Half-street and full-street reciprocal-range games introduce sequential decisions; UTG versus BB then tests what changes when realistic positions and ranges replace tiny games.

Evidence: [PDF p. 90](../../../sources/pdf/Play%20Optimal%20Poker.pdf#page=90) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/play-optimal-poker/pages-0089-0096.md#pdf-page-90)

### Exploitative process

The four-step process in chapter 5 starts from an equilibrium reference, identifies an opponent mistake, changes one's strategy to profit, and considers how the opponent may adjust back.

Evidence: [PDF p. 126](../../../sources/pdf/Play%20Optimal%20Poker.pdf#page=126) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/play-optimal-poker/pages-0121-0128.md#pdf-page-126)

### Complex ranges, raising, synthesis

The Ace-to-Five model adds more hand classes; raising introduces another strategic branch and blockers refine the final combined examples. Work the chapter tests against the original diagrams.

Evidence: [PDF p. 156](../../../sources/pdf/Play%20Optimal%20Poker.pdf#page=156) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/play-optimal-poker/pages-0153-0160.md#pdf-page-156)
