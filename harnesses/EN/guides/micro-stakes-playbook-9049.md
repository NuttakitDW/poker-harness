## Explained study themes

### Opponent taxonomy and preflop

Williams distinguishes bad and good regulars from whales and maniacs, then builds opening, stealing, three-bet defense, and four-bet plans around who is in the pot.

Evidence: [PDF p. 47](../../../sources/pdf/9049_the_micro_stakes_playbook.pdf#page=47) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/micro-stakes-playbook-9049/pages-0041-0048.md#pdf-page-47)

### Flop and turn pressure

Flop chapters cover c-bets, floats, raises, and three-bet pots. Turn chapters add second barrels, double floats, and bluff raises; choose candidates using range and opponent evidence.

Evidence: [PDF p. 93](../../../sources/pdf/9049_the_micro_stakes_playbook.pdf#page=93) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/micro-stakes-playbook-9049/pages-0089-0096.md#pdf-page-93)

### River choices

The river playbook separates triple barrels, stop-and-go value, thin raises, and bluff catches. The key comparison is worse hands that call versus better hands that remain.

Evidence: [PDF p. 181](../../../sources/pdf/9049_the_micro_stakes_playbook.pdf#page=181) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/micro-stakes-playbook-9049/pages-0177-0184.md#pdf-page-181)

### Stop-and-go bluff and river raise

Chapters 14–15 add a delayed bluff and river bluff raise. These depend on a credible prior line and an opponent able to fold; the dramatic chapter labels are not evidence of automatic profit.

Evidence: [PDF p. 181](../../../sources/pdf/9049_the_micro_stakes_playbook.pdf#page=181) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/micro-stakes-playbook-9049/pages-0177-0184.md#pdf-page-181)

### Professional routine

Final chapters cover study, leak fixing, HUD setup, table selection, bankroll planning, variance, and tilt. These are process suggestions, not guaranteed income or current site guidance.
