## Explained study themes

### Purpose, habits, and systems

Cardner connects a clear reason to play with goals, small repeatable habits, daily plans, tracking, and implementation intentions. The activities ask the reader to turn vague ambition into observable routines.

Evidence: [PDF p. 26](../../../sources/pdf/Peak%20Poker%20Performance.pdf#page=26) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/peak-poker-performance/pages-0025-0032.md#pdf-page-26)

### A-game and attention

The A-game chapter defines one's strongest performance, flow, situational awareness, pregame preparation, and responses to strong emotion; multitasking is treated as a cost to focus.

Evidence: [PDF p. 87](../../../sources/pdf/Peak%20Poker%20Performance.pdf#page=87) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/peak-poker-performance/pages-0081-0088.md#pdf-page-87)

### Psychological hurdles

The book traces tilt and other leaks through emotional awareness and learned responses, then offers exercises to name triggers and interrupt unhelpful thought patterns.

Evidence: [PDF p. 113](../../../sources/pdf/Peak%20Poker%20Performance.pdf#page=113) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/peak-poker-performance/pages-0113-0120.md#pdf-page-113)

### Read as workbook

Chapter highlights, observation activities, and Jonathan Little's comments are part of the instructional design; record an action and review it rather than reading as inspirational prose alone.

Evidence: [PDF p. 41](../../../sources/pdf/Peak%20Poker%20Performance.pdf#page=41) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/peak-poker-performance/pages-0041-0048.md#pdf-page-41)
