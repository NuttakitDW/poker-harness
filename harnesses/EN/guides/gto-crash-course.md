## Explained study themes

### Basic game theory and AKQ

The first video sections explain equilibrium with a tiny ace-king-queen game, then show how a known deviation creates an exploit.

Evidence: [PDF p. 10](../../../sources/pdf/GTO/GTO%20Crash%20Course%20Strategy%20Guide%20-%20Red%20Chip%20Poker.pdf#page=10) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/gto-crash-course/pages-0009-0016.md#pdf-page-10)

### A–5 model and range building

The expanded model has more hand classes, allowing value, bluff, and bluff-catcher interactions that a three-card game cannot show.

Evidence: [PDF p. 20](../../../sources/pdf/GTO/GTO%20Crash%20Course%20Strategy%20Guide%20-%20Red%20Chip%20Poker.pdf#page=20) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/gto-crash-course/pages-0017-0024.md#pdf-page-20)

### Raising and exploitation in A–5

The later toy-game lessons add raising and revisit how a known opponent deviation changes optimal responses. A strategy is conditional on the model's available actions and payoffs.

Evidence: [PDF p. 36](../../../sources/pdf/GTO/GTO%20Crash%20Course%20Strategy%20Guide%20-%20Red%20Chip%20Poker.pdf#page=36) · [extracted page](../../EN/sources/gto-crash-course/pages-0033-0040.md#pdf-page-36)
