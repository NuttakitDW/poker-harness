The question

Do Hwang's tiers match a solver at 20bb?

Jeff Hwang sorts PLO starting hands into Premium, Speculative, Marginal and Trash. Our classifier implements those tiers from ranks, gaps and suits: 5.49% of the 270,725 hands are Premium, 13.83% Speculative, 19.91% Marginal and 60.76% Trash.

We asked three things for the first voluntary action of a six-max hand at 20 big blinds: which hands the solver raises, limps or folds at each seat; where Hwang's grading disagrees; and whether a few new archetypes describe the solver better.

“The premium-class hands can usually be played from any position and under most circumstances.” Hwang also says to usually limp up front, and to play Marginal hands only late for the minimum. His book targets deep, multi-way cash games.

Method

A full 20bb game, checked hand by hand

The game

Six-max, 20bb, 0.5/1 blinds, no ante, no rake. Pot-sized raises up to a four-bet shove; pot-sized bet plus two raises on flop, turn and river. 232,258 decisions. Two independent seeds, {{Deals}}M deals each.

The check

Preflop buckets never mix Hwang tiers. For {{AuditClasses}} exact hand classes at every seat we compared fold, limp and raise with the solved strategy played afterwards. Three standard errors separate enter, fold and too close.

Result 2

What decides raise, limp or fold

Play = the solver's chance of limping or raising. Entry EV = the better of limp and raise minus fold, in big blinds, from the per-hand audit.

{{DRIVERS}}
  • Big cards first. Zero or one card Ten or higher is almost never played early. Three big cards are played 69% under the gun and 96% on the button.
  • The Ace suit beats the second suit. A suited Ace moves UTG entry value from −0.45 to +0.24. Low-suited hands play almost like rainbow ones.
  • Pairs follow rank. KK and QQ are profitable everywhere, JJ and TT only from the cutoff on, small pairs only from the small blind.
  • Connectivity alone does little. Rundowns are played only 35% UTG and lose on average. They pay when they also carry big cards and suits.
  • Limp or raise. Aces and nut big cards are raised. Big pairs are limped about 60% UTG, though limp and raise differ by only 0.1bb. Broadway rundowns are the one group where limping UTG is clearly better.

Result 3

Where Hwang disagrees

{{MISMATCH}}

Premium straight hands, UTG: entry minus fold (bb)

{{HEAT}}
  • Straight and pair-plus-connector hands are overrated. Single-suited 9-9-8-7 types and gapped straights without big cards lose about 0.35–0.7bb against folding UTG. They need deep stacks.
  • Big-card hands with a dangler are underrated. Big pairs and Broadway cards with a rag are Marginal to Hwang, yet the solver plays most of them UTG; the double-suited ones win +0.17 to +0.66bb.
  • Suit quality is too coarse. Any two cards of a suit count as suited. The solver separates a suited Ace, a suited King to Ten, and low suits, and plays three-flush hands almost as rarely as rainbow ones.
  • The Premium folds. Single-suited, three-flush and monotone rundowns from 6532 to 9876 lose 0.4–1.3bb against folding UTG. Double-suited rundowns and ones headed by two big cards are fine.

The proposal

{{ArchCount}} archetypes for 20bb first-in play

Check the rules in order; the first match wins. A big card is a Ten or higher and a big pair counts twice. Actions come from the audited values of limping and raising; “mixed” means the better entry is within 0.10bb of folding.

{{EVALUATION}}

From under the gun to the cutoff, the archetypes lose about a third as much EV as Hwang's advice, and half as much on the button. They come within a few hundredths of a big blind of the solver's own 780-bucket strategy. Every grouping struggles in the small blind, where most hands are close to break-even. Limits

What this does not show

  • One depth (20bb), pot-sized bets only, no rake, no antes. Deeper stacks favour Hwang's straight hands; the archetypes should be re-derived there.
  • Postflop hands are grouped, not tracked exactly, and multiplayer CFR has no equilibrium guarantee. The exploitability evidence is only the small gain (at most about 0.05bb) from changing the first action.
  • The archetypes were designed on the same solve that evaluates them. The held-out split protects each group's action, not the rules themselves.
  • Non-Premium tiers were checked on 400 hands each, so many are too close to call.
